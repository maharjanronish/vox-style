Tucked between India and China sits one of the oldest countries on Earth: Nepal.

Legend says the Kathmandu Valley was once a lake, cut open by a sage's sword.

Around five hundred and sixty-three B.C., the Buddha was born here, in the plains of Lumbini.

For a thousand years, Licchavi and Malla kings carved cities of wood and brick.

Then in seventeen forty-three, a hill prince named Prithvi Narayan Shah set out to unite them all.

By seventeen sixty-eight, dozens of rival kingdoms had become a single country: Nepal.

In eighteen fourteen, the British attacked — but the Gurkhas fought so fiercely, Nepal was never colonized.

Inside, the Rana family ruled for one hundred and four years while kings watched from their palace.

In nineteen fifty-three, climbers finally stood on the roof of the world — Everest, eight thousand eight hundred and forty-nine metres.

And in two thousand and eight, Nepal ended two hundred and forty years of monarchy and became a republic.

Never colonized. Always rebuilding. The story of Nepal.
