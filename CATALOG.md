# Video Studio — Catalog

Auto-generated by `npm run catalog` from each prop's own `meta`. Do not edit this file; edit `tools/catalog.header.md` for the intro.

You write ONE file: `projects/<name>/script.json` (narration lines, shots, props, data). Everything on screen is drawn in code by the props listed below. Never invent props, slots, options or cue forms. If nothing fits, use the closest props and end your reply with a **MISSING PROPS** list (name, what it should show, which shot).

## Script format
- `lines[]`: the narration. `{ "id", "text", "say"?, "caps"? }`.
  - `text` is the written line. `say` (optional) is what is spoken when it differs (numbers as words, pronunciations). **Spoken text = `say` if present, else `text`.** All word cues match the spoken text.
  - `caps` controls captions: `|` splits chunks, `*word*` highlights a word, `"caps": false` hides captions for that line. Keep chunks to ≤ 6 words.
- `shots[]`: visuals. Shots tile the timeline (a shot ends where the next starts; the first starts at 0; the video has a 0.8 s tail).
  - A shot that shares its line with other shots needs `span: [fromCue, toCue]`.
  - A shot may carry `camera`, `layers`, `sfx`, `transition: { type, duration?, slots?, options? }`. `transition.type` is a prop from the transitions category.
  - See "Complete working example" for exactly how a shot refers to its line.
- A layer: `{ prop, slots?, options?, at?, until?, depth?, z?, space?, enter?, exit?, id? }`.
  - `slots` = the content (text, photo, data). `options` = look and placement.
  - `at` / `until` are cues. Without `until` the layer is cut at shot end; with `until` it fades out there.
  - `at: "end"` places the layer at shot end minus the prop's default duration.
  - `depth` 0 (far) … 1 (near) gives camera parallax. `space` is `"world"` (moves with camera) or `"screen"`.
  - `enter` / `exit` are optional animation overrides; normally leave them out.
- Cues: `"start"`, `"end"`, `"t:1.5"` (seconds into the shot's line), `"word:kong"`, `"word:the#2"` (second "the"), each with an optional offset such as `"+0.2s"` / `"-0.1s"`. A word that is not in the spoken text is an error.
- Camera moves: push-in, pull-out, pan-left, pan-right, pan-up, pan-down, drift, tilt, shake, hold. Use a slow push-in on most shots.
- Positions: most props accept `x`, `y`, `w`, `h` in pixels or as `"50%"` strings; position is the **centre**; `rot` is in degrees.
- Draw order: paper → world layers (camera + depth) → screen layers → film grain → transitions → captions.

## Formats
9:16 is the default (1080×1920, safe margins top 150 / bottom 480 / sides 70; captions sit in the bottom band). 16:9 is 1920×1080 (safe top 70 / bottom 230 / sides 110).

## Pacing and taste
- A new visual or cue every 2–3 s. At most 2 text-heavy props on screen at once.
- Entrances are quick ease-outs (150–400 ms). Overshoot only on stamps and pins.
- Palette: warm paper, one ink colour, two accents (tomato, cobalt), one highlight yellow. Leave colour options out unless you need them.
- One idea per shot. Prefer a photo or a big number over a paragraph.

## Sound effects (`shot.sfx`)
`"sfx": ["whoosh"]` or `[{ "type": "thud", "at": "word:agreed", "gain": 0.8 }]`. `at` uses the same cue grammar as layers (`start`, `end`, `t:1.2`, `word:x`, optional `+0.2s`); `t:` is relative to the shot start.
Known sounds: `whoosh`, `slide`, `paper`, `swipe`, `thud`, `tick`, `pop`, `scribble`. Unknown names fall back to `tick` with a warning.
Automatic (no need to write): stamp→thud, newspaperClip/paperPhoto/baseMap→paper, highlighter→swipe, markerCircle→scribble, bigNumber/barChart→pop, lowerThird→slide, wipe→whoosh, paperSlide→slide. Disable with `npm run sfx -- <project> --no-auto`.

## Writing narration lines
Digits are fine ("1997", "6.5", "1st"). Use `say` only for pronunciation overrides. Aim for 8–12 words per line, one idea per line. Cue words (`word:x`) must be spoken in that line's text and must not be filler words.

## Reference

A `?` after a type means optional. Slots are content; options are look and placement.

#### arrow

**Use:** A hand-drawn marker arrow that draws itself and points at something: a face in a photo, a number, a place on a map. x and y are where the tip lands; dir is the direction it points (0 right, 90 down, -90 up).

**Do not:** More than two at once, or arrows with no clear target. Use callout when the arrow needs a label.

_default duration 2.5s · space: world · default depth: 0.8_
